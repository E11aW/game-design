## Common Media Types
### Music & Sound Effects
To really immerse players into the gameworld, a lot of RPGs utilize sound. This starts with the background music that helps convey the feel or ‘vibe’ of the game. Music that leans more eerie vs. more light and playful creates very different environments for players to explore. Adding various sound effects also help bring players into the action of the game, understanding that they are walking around as indicated by footsteps, or attacking with a sword swinging sound. 

These audio cues can be implemented into a game via audio assets/files. These can then be triggered by events in-game. The music would likely trigger on load, while a sword-swinging sound effect would activate when the player hits attack. Event-triggered sound has the pros of being flexible and integrated only when necessary. The only downside would be having to track more of the player’s actions if they weren’t tracked already. 
### Animations 
I love animation. And the simplicity of animation in 2D pixel art games is visually very appealing. Even at such a small scale a lot of movement can be shown through walk or attack animations. Even simple idle animations can bring a lot of personality to the game. In my team’s current prototype our player character doesn’t even have a simple walking animation since it is just a placeholder but the lack of animation really takes away from the clarity of player movement. This goes to show just how important animation is in conveying motion in a video game.

Most animation in this style of games comes from the use of multiple sprites. One example of this is having a list of sprites the player character cycles through when walking, creating a walking animation for the player sprite. The same can be done for attack animations that are played when players attack. The pros are definitely the simplicity of integrating it into the game, but drawing so many sprites by hand can certainly be time consuming, especially for smoother animations. At least pixel art limits the number of sprites you can realistically create.
## Media Integration
I doubt our game will have any cutscenes in it (a very common form of media integration) but possibly at the very end when the player beats the game. The main way we will integrate media will likely be through files; adding audio files for music and sound and effects, and additional sprites for players, enemies, and interactable objects. I believe this will be the simplest way to get the type of media we need into our game, also allowing us to control what media goes where in our game. 

In general I am quite excited to integrate media into our game as it will really help bring our dungeon to life!
