# Checkpoint 3: Game Test Summary
By Ella Williams
## Questions for Game Tester
1. What is your favorite part of the game so far?
2. What is your least favorite part of the game so far?
3. What could still be improved?
4. Is there anything that takes you out of the game? What is it?
5. What does the game still need to feel truly polished?
6. Do you have any other suggestions?
## Feedback
### Game Tester 1 - Paige
Paige's favorite part of the game was the player sprite, noting that she "liked its weird nose." She was the main one playing the game, testing out different mechanics and getting a little confused by the fact that the save system does not update player location correctly. She managed to accidentally end the game by saving too close to the door and immediately ending the game afterwards. She encountered a similar bug where game over no longer works, just resetting the player health instead. 

Her final suggestions for the game were to improve the overall playability by either changing the music or allowing it to be adjusted (it got annoying over time) and increasing the speed of the homescreen typing since that was extremely slow.
### Game Tester 2 - Michelle
Michelle similarly really liked the sprites, specifically enjoying the pumpkin as an enemy and how it attacks you. She also really liked the background but really wanted the little joystick on the ground to be interactable, suggesting it could maybe open the door or even be used to solve some sort of puzzle. She also suggested that more enemies or levels would make the game much more engaging, and that it would be nice to have stats be a little clearer. The testers quickly noticed how the stat saving is broken and the awesome item does not save stat changes. 

As a final note, Michelle quite liked the "Do you want to take this awesome item?" question, and immediately knew I was the one who added that in.
### Game Tester 3 - John
As was the theme, John liked the background sprites, specifically the slime oozing out of the wall. He also believed that combat would be greatly improved by adding an attack noise to indicate when player strikes, and when the enemy strikes since as of right now there is no real indication other than the player health going down. This made the item button quite confusing since it is not implemented yet but the enemy still strikes back. 

Since items are not implemented yet, the explanation at the start of the game is also a little misleading since it mentions players being able to select defensive options during battle, but those do not exist yet. He suggested either implementing items into combat or removing mentions of defensive options from the guide explanation to be more consistent with the actual game.
## Summary of Findings
Overall it was very clear to the game testers that the game was not finished, not only did the lack of game bounds take the testers out of the game, but we also managed to fully break the start button, making the game unplayable. The general consensus for game positives were the various sprites and background, though testers did state that having all objects interactable would be better. Most of the suggestions involved adding in mechanics/objects that were already planned for development, so I believe the game will be much more playable once those are added in.
## Team Summary
Across all three sets of testers, several consistent themes appeared during the playtesting process. Players liked the visual style of the game, and many mentioned that the sprites, backgrounds, and basic movement felt smooth and enjoyable. Testers also said the controls were easy to understand, even for people who do not usually play RPG-style games. This shows that the core layout and basic gameplay ideas are working well.

However, every tester also pointed out that the game felt unfinished in its current state. Many mechanics did not work as expected, which made the experience feel incomplete. Testers noticed issues such as the lever not functioning, the character resetting too often, missing game boundaries, and collisions that did not behave correctly. They also said the battle sequence needed clearer timing, stronger feedback, and more polished animations. These missing features and bugs made it hard for players to stay focused on the main objective.

Testers gave several suggestions that matched ideas the team already plans to add. Many players wanted more interactable objects and simple enemies like skeletons or goblins. Others recommended clearer attack visuals and better background choices during fights. Even though their ideas varied, all testers agreed that the game would improve a lot once these planned mechanics are added.

Overall, the combined feedback from all teammates showed the same conclusion. The game has a strong foundation with good visuals, smooth movement, and easy controls, but it needs more polish and working systems to feel complete. Testers believe the project has potential, and with bug fixes, finished mechanics, and clearer feedback, it can become a much more engaging and enjoyable game.
